RemitBridge

Privacy policy

Effective October 1, 2026

RemitBridge is a free app that compares the cost of sending money abroad. It is run by Angad Kochar as a personal project. This policy covers the RemitBridge mobile app and this site. In short: comparing prices needs no account, we don't sell data, and there is no advertising or third-party tracking.

Without an account

You can compare prices without signing up. The app still records how it's used, to count users and see which features help. Each record holds:

  • a random ID created when the app is first opened, which is not linked to your name, device or advertising ID;
  • what happened, such as "comparison run", with the choices involved, such as the route and the amount you checked;
  • whether you're on iOS or Android, the app version, and the time.

We don't collect your location, contacts, device identifiers or advertising ID, and we don't track you across other apps or websites.

With an account

An account is optional. If you create one, we also store:

  • your email address, or the account details Apple or Google share if you sign in with them;
  • the name you give in Account, or the one Apple or Google share, used only to greet you in the app;
  • anything you choose to save, such as comparisons and transfers you enter yourself (provider, amounts, rate, date and any note);
  • the usage records above, linked to your account while you're signed in.

Saved items are private to your account. Database rules stop anyone else from reading them.

Rate alerts

If you turn on a rate alert, we keep the route, the amount, how you send and pay, your target, your language, and your phone's notification token (a code that lets a notification reach this app on your phone), with your account. After each price update we check your alert, and when a price reaches your target we send one notification, at most once a day. If you choose WhatsApp instead (only once you've linked it, below), the alert comes as a WhatsApp message and no notification token is kept. Asking for notifications happens only when you turn an alert on. Delete an alert in the app, or delete your account, and it's gone.

Scanning a receipt

The app asks for camera or photo access only when you scan a transfer receipt, and uses only the photo you take or choose. It reads the receipt on your phone first. Before anything leaves your phone, it covers names, addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, and card, account and reference numbers with solid boxes, removes the photo's hidden details (such as where it was taken), and shows you exactly what would be sent. You can cover more before sending.

What's sent is the covered photo and the text read from it. Our server checks the text again and covers anything the phone missed; if it finds anything, it doesn't use or keep the photo. What we keep is the values read (such as the amounts, fee, tax, exchange rate, currencies, date, provider and how you paid), where each came from, and when. We don't keep the text, and we keep the photo only if you choose to. A scanned receipt carries no account, phone number or device ID.

On WhatsApp, if you send a photo of a receipt, our server reads it and covers the same personal details before the values are read; only the covered text goes on to be read, never the photo. The photo is deleted once it's read and is never stored. To send you the answer a few minutes later, we keep your number encrypted until the answer is sent, and never longer than an hour.

A scanned receipt is deleted after 30 days, and you can delete it sooner in the app. If you turn on "Keep this receipt for research", we keep the covered photo and the values, to study what people really pay, and report them only as totals. The app shows those totals to everyone, and only for groups of at least five receipts, so no single receipt can be picked out.

WhatsApp

If you message our WhatsApp bot, we keep a scrambled ID made from your number (it can't be turned back into the number), your reply language, and the last route you asked about. If you link WhatsApp to your account, from Account in the app, we connect that scrambled ID to your account, so comparisons you send with SAVE are kept there, and we keep your WhatsApp number encrypted so we can send you the rate alerts you choose to get on WhatsApp. Only the server that sends WhatsApp messages can unlock it; it never appears in our logs and goes to no one but WhatsApp. Send UNLINK, or tap Unlink in the app, and the number and your WhatsApp alerts are deleted at once; deleting your account or sending STOP does the same. Send STOP and we delete everything we keep about your number.

Asking for a route

If you ask us to check prices for a country we don't cover yet, we keep the two countries, how you'd like the money to arrive if you said, and when you asked. If you give an email address so we can tell you when it's ready, we send that one email and then delete the address; if we decide we can't add the route, we delete it then. Delete your account and your requests go with it.

Keeping it safe

  • Everything travels encrypted, and the database encrypts what it stores.
  • On your phone, your sign-in is stored encrypted, with a key kept in the phone's secure storage (the iPhone Keychain or Android Keystore) that never goes into a backup or onto another phone.
  • A receipt photo is deleted from the app's storage as soon as its text is read; after that the app works only from the covered copy.
  • There's no password to steal: you sign in with a code we email you, or with Apple or Google.
  • To stop abuse, we count requests from each network address, and receipt checks from each phone (by a random ID the app makes, which says nothing about you), each account, and each WhatsApp chat. We keep only a scrambled form of the address or ID, which can't be turned back into it and changes every day, and we delete the counts within a day.
  • The deletions promised here, receipts after 30 days and a WhatsApp number within the hour, run on a timer.

Why we use it

  • To run the app: signing you in and keeping what you save.
  • To measure and improve it: counting users, comparisons and features used. We only report these as totals, never about an individual.

We don't sell or rent your data, and we don't use it for advertising.

Who processes it

  • Supabase hosts the database and sign-in, in the United States.
  • Resend sends the sign-in code email, if you sign in with email.
  • Apple or Google handle sign-in if you choose them.
  • Expo's push service, with Apple or Google, delivers rate alert notifications. They get the notification token and the message (for example, the provider and the amount), nothing else.
  • Groq runs an AI model (currently Alibaba's Qwen) on a scanned receipt when the app can't read a value on its own. It gets only the covered photo and text, never the original. Groq says it doesn't keep what it's sent, except briefly when investigating errors or abuse, and doesn't train on it.

To check a receipt, our server also asks Frankfurter, a public exchange-rate service, for the rate on the receipt's date. That request holds only the two currencies and the date.

Each processes data only to provide its service to RemitBridge.

Deleting your data

In the app, go to Account, then Delete account. This deletes your account and everything saved to it at once. Usage records made while you were signed in are kept only as anonymous totals: the link to your account is removed. You can also email us to ask for deletion.

Children

RemitBridge is not directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect their data.

Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update the date at the top. Significant changes will be noted in the app.
