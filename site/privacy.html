Privacy policy

Last updated October 12, 2026

RemitBridge is a free app that compares the cost of sending money abroad. {{LEGAL_ENTITY}} ("RemitBridge Labs", "we") publishes and operates it, and is responsible for the data described here. No person who founded, runs or volunteers for RemitBridge Labs holds your data in their own name. This policy covers the RemitBridge mobile app, this site and the RemitBridge WhatsApp bot.

In short

  • {{LEGAL_NAME}} does not send, hold or exchange money. RemitBridge only shows prices.
  • Nothing in the app, on this site or from the bot is financial or legal advice.
  • We have no affiliate or paid relationships with providers. Providers are ranked by the amount received, and nothing else.
  • Prices come from providers' public websites, provider APIs used with the provider's permission (such as Wise's), prices entered by RemitBridge staff, and mid-market rates from Frankfurter. Prices change during the day, so one may be out of date.
  • Comparing prices needs no account. We don't sell data, and there is no advertising or third-party tracking.

Without an account

You can compare prices without signing up. The app still records how it's used, to count users and see which features help. Each record holds:

  • a random ID created when the app is first opened, which is not linked to your name, device or advertising ID;
  • what happened, such as "comparison run", with the choices involved, such as the route and the amount you checked;
  • whether you're on iOS or Android, the app version, and the time.

We don't collect your location, contacts, device identifiers or advertising ID, and we don't track you across other apps or websites.

With an account

An account is optional. If you create one, we also store:

  • your email address, or the account details Apple or Google share if you sign in with them;
  • the name you give in Account, or the one Apple or Google share, used only to greet you in the app;
  • anything you choose to save, such as comparisons and transfers you enter yourself (provider, amounts, rate, date and any note);
  • the usage records above, linked to your account while you're signed in.

Saved items are private to your account. Database rules stop anyone else from reading them.

Rate alerts

If you turn on a rate alert, we keep the route, the amount, how you send and pay, your target, your language, and your phone's notification token (a code that lets a notification reach this app on your phone), with your account. After each price update we check your alert, and when a price reaches your target we send one notification, at most once a day. Asking for notifications happens only when you turn an alert on. Delete an alert in the app, or delete your account, and it's gone.

Scanning a receipt

The app asks for camera or photo access only when you scan a transfer receipt, and uses only the photo you take or choose. It reads the receipt on your phone first. Before anything leaves your phone, it covers names, addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, and card, account and reference numbers with solid boxes, removes the photo's hidden details (such as where it was taken), and shows you exactly what would be sent. You can cover more before sending.

What's sent is the covered photo and the text read from it. Our server checks the text again and covers anything the phone missed. If it finds anything, it doesn't use or keep the photo.

Our server reads the transaction figures from the covered text: the amounts, fee, tax, exchange rate, currencies, date, provider and how you paid. When its own rules can't read a figure, it sends the covered text, and in some cases the covered photo, to an AI model run by Groq, a third-party provider. Zero data retention is turned on for our Groq account, so Groq doesn't store what it's sent. The app asks your permission before your first receipt is sent, and says on every scan that this can happen. If you don't allow it, nothing is sent.

  • The text read from the receipt is never stored.
  • The figures, where each came from, and when, are kept for 30 days and then deleted. You can delete them sooner in the app.
  • The photo is never stored, unless you turn on "Keep this receipt for research". Then we keep the covered photo and the figures for 24 months, to study what people really pay, and delete them after that. We report research receipts only as totals, and only for groups of at least five receipts, so no single receipt can be picked out.

A scanned receipt carries no account, phone number or device ID. The app adds up what your scans say you could have kept, on your phone only; that total never leaves it, and deleting a receipt in the app takes it out.

On WhatsApp, if you send a photo of a receipt, our server reads it and covers the same personal details before any figures are read. Only the covered text goes on to be read, including by Groq, never the photo. The photo is deleted once it's read and is never stored. How we handle your number while it's read is below.

WhatsApp

When you message our WhatsApp bot, we don't store your phone number. We keep a scrambled ID made from it (a keyed hash, which can't be turned back into the number), your reply language, and the last route you asked about.

We keep the number itself only while a receipt photo is read: to send you the answer a few minutes later, we keep it encrypted until the answer is sent, and never longer than an hour. Only the server that sends WhatsApp messages can unlock it. It never appears in our logs and goes to no one but WhatsApp.

When the bot can't read a message with its own rules (a question, an idea, or a request written in your own words), it sends the text of that message, and nothing else, to an AI model run by Cloudflare, to work out what you're asking and answer from RemitBridge's own information. Cloudflare doesn't get your number, and doesn't use what it's sent to train AI models. If you send a suggestion for the app, we keep its text, its language and the date, with no number or ID attached.

If you link WhatsApp from the WhatsApp tab in the app, we connect the scrambled ID (not your number) to your account, so comparisons you send with SAVE are kept there. Send UNLINK, or tap Unlink in the app, and the link is removed at once; deleting your account does the same. Send STOP and we delete everything we keep about your number.

Asking for a route

If you ask us to check prices for a country we don't cover yet, we keep the two countries, how you'd like the money to arrive if you said, and when you asked. If you give an email address so we can tell you when it's ready, we send that one email and then delete the address; if we decide we can't add the route, we delete it then. Delete your account and your requests go with it.

What we never collect

We never ask for or collect immigration status or documents, bank or card sign-in details, passwords or PINs, or digital wallet details.

Keeping it safe

  • Everything travels encrypted, and the database encrypts what it stores.
  • On your phone, your sign-in is stored encrypted, with a key kept in the phone's secure storage (the iPhone Keychain or Android Keystore) that never goes into a backup or onto another phone.
  • A receipt photo is deleted from the app's storage as soon as its text is read; after that the app works only from the covered copy.
  • There's no password to steal: you sign in with a code we email you, or with Apple or Google.
  • To stop abuse, we count requests from each network address, and receipt checks from each phone (by the random ID above), each account, and each WhatsApp chat. We keep only a scrambled form of the address or ID, which can't be turned back into it and changes every day, and we delete the counts within a day.
  • The deletions promised here run automatically: receipts after 30 days (24 months for research receipts), and a WhatsApp number held for a receipt within the hour.

Why we use it

  • To run the app: signing you in and keeping what you save.
  • To measure and improve it: counting users, comparisons and features used. We only report these as totals, never about an individual.

We don't sell or rent your data, and we don't use it for advertising.

Who processes it

  • Supabase hosts the database and sign-in, in the United States.
  • Resend sends the sign-in code email, if you sign in with email.
  • Apple or Google handle sign-in if you choose them.
  • Expo's push service, with Apple or Google, delivers rate alert notifications. They get the notification token and the message (for example, the provider and the amount), nothing else.
  • Groq runs an AI model (currently Alibaba's Qwen) on a scanned receipt when our rules can't read a figure. It gets only the covered text, and in some cases the covered photo, never the original. Zero data retention is on, and Groq doesn't train on it.
  • Cloudflare runs an AI model (Workers AI) on the text of a WhatsApp message our bot's rules can't read. It gets only that text, never your number, and doesn't use it to train AI models.
  • Meta carries the messages you exchange with the WhatsApp bot, under WhatsApp's own terms.
  • GitHub runs the program that reads a receipt photo sent on WhatsApp. The photo is held only in that program's memory, on a temporary machine, and is never written to disk.
  • Vercel hosts this site.

To check a receipt, our server also asks Frankfurter, a public exchange-rate service, for the rate on the receipt's date. That request holds only the two currencies and the date.

Each processes data only to provide its service to {{LEGAL_NAME}}.

Deleting your data

In the app, go to Account, then Delete account. This deletes your account and everything saved to it at once. If you signed in with Apple, it also ends RemitBridge's access to your Apple Account. Usage records made while you were signed in are kept only as anonymous totals: the link to your account is removed. You can also email us to ask for deletion.

Children

RemitBridge is not directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect their data.
